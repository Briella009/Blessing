# Publication brief

## Core public-interest problem

Africa's AI incident record is fragmented across fact-checks, newsrooms, company notices, court files, peer-reviewed deployment studies and global incident databases. Fragmentation makes comparison difficult and can hide patterns that matter for governance, security and public accountability.

## v0.2.0 findings

AAIO v0.2.0 contains **19 source-traceable core incidents across nine primary African countries**. The dataset remains conservative and still reflects what is easiest to observe publicly, but it now extends beyond synthetic media and public-information harms into two operational settings:

- a peer-reviewed Kenyan clinical decision-support evaluation that documented actively harmful LLM recommendations; and
- South African judicial material documenting professional reliance on fictitious ChatGPT-generated legal authorities.

This matters because the original visibility skew remains real. Deepfakes, election/influence incidents, scams and public chatbot failures leave searchable traces. Internal failures in hospitals, lenders, workplaces, classrooms and government systems may be harder to observe even when their consequences are more direct.

The missing data is therefore not just an inconvenience. It is part of the research problem.

## What makes AAIO different

- It preserves source links and uncertainty instead of flattening every claim into a fact.
- It separates evidence confidence from impact severity.
- It keeps important non-qualifying automated-system cases on a watchlist rather than mislabelling them as AI incidents.
- It preserves multilingual source candidates and translation uncertainty outside the core until promotion is justified.
- It records operational-to-core promotion provenance instead of erasing the evidence-review stage.
- It keeps an append-only material-change history for substantive record revisions.
- It includes deterministic AIID/OECD-oriented interoperability exports while explicitly flagging non-equivalent fields.
- It is designed to complement global repositories, not claim to replace or represent them.

## Claims that should NOT be made

Do not say AAIO is comprehensive, representative of all African countries, endorsed by AIID/OECD/NIST/African Union, or proof that one country has more AI incidents than another. Record counts are influenced by reporting visibility, language, sector transparency and research coverage.

Do not convert unsafe model outputs into unsupported patient-injury counts, or attribute all litigation outcomes to a generative-AI tool when the evidence supports only a narrower professional-use failure.

## Suggested publication thesis

**Responsible AI needs institutional memory. Africa cannot learn systematically from AI failures that are never structured as evidence — and a credible evidence layer must preserve what is known, what is uncertain, and what is still missing.**
