# Research agenda

AAIO is a growing evidence infrastructure project, not a claim of comprehensive African coverage. v0.2.0 establishes multilingual discovery, operational evidence staging, OECD/AIID semantic interoperability, promotion provenance and material record history. The next research phase should focus on evidence depth and external use rather than simply adding rows.

## Priority research areas

1. **Under-observed operational sectors** — continue searching for source-traceable deployed failures in credit and financial decisioning, employment and recruitment, education, public-service delivery and critical infrastructure. Do not lower the AI-linkage threshold to fill sector quotas.
2. **African-language evidence** — expand beyond the current French-, Arabic- and Portuguese-language starter layer and recruit reviewers who can assess original-language evidence without relying entirely on machine-assisted translation.
3. **Longitudinal incident outcomes** — revisit core records when courts, regulators, deployers, platforms or affected communities publish follow-up evidence. Record material changes in `data/record_history.csv` rather than silently replacing prior interpretations.
4. **Upstream validation** — submit original qualifying AAIO cases to AIID and other compatible public incident infrastructures. Track accepted, merged or rejected submissions as external evidence rather than assuming interoperability equals adoption.
5. **Affected-community evidence** — where ethical and publicly documented, capture how incidents affected people and institutions rather than describing only the technical failure or media artifact.
6. **Incident-response outcomes** — distinguish detection, acknowledgement, mitigation, remediation, compensation, appeal, decommissioning and unresolved status. This can make AAIO useful for studying not only failures, but also what happens after failures become visible.
7. **Cross-border representation** — improve how one event affecting multiple African jurisdictions is represented without double-counting incidents.
8. **Reporting-bias measurement** — study how language, press freedom, litigation visibility, platform transparency and sector secrecy shape what becomes observable in public incident datasets.
9. **Stable research citation** — archive versioned releases in a DOI-minting repository and maintain a concept-level citation path for the evolving project.
10. **Reuse and reproducibility** — prioritize independently verifiable citations, contributors, upstream acceptances, presentations and dataset reuse. Record those in `docs/impact.md`.

## Research questions AAIO is positioned to study

- Which African AI harms are easiest to observe publicly, and which are structurally hidden?
- How does evidence confidence change when primary judicial, regulatory or peer-reviewed sources become available?
- Do multilingual discovery methods materially change the geographic and harm-type distribution of visible incidents?
- How often do documented incidents lead to meaningful remediation rather than only public acknowledgement?
- Which fields map cleanly to global reporting frameworks, and which locally important context is lost during standardisation?

The project's success should be measured less by the number of rows than by the **quality, traceability, correction discipline and external reuse of the evidence**.
